## How the interest-free period actually works

The 45-day figure is the maximum, not the norm, and it is the single most
misunderstood number on any credit card in this market.

The period runs from the start of a statement cycle to the payment due date. A
purchase made on the **first day** of the cycle gets close to the full 45 days.
A purchase made on the **last day** gets only the gap between statement date and
due date - typically around 15 days. Customers who plan around "45 days" and
spend late in the cycle are surprised by interest they did not expect.

The interest-free period also applies only when the statement balance is paid in
**full**. Paying the minimum keeps the account current but ends the interest-free
treatment, and interest is then charged on the average daily balance - which
includes the days before the statement was issued, not just the days after the
due date. Cash advances are outside the interest-free period entirely and accrue
from the day they are taken.

## Reading the 0.5% cashback against the annual fee

At 0.5% cashback and a VND 800,000 annual fee, the card returns more than it
costs at roughly VND 160 million of annual spending, before considering the
waiver threshold or the birthday voucher. That arithmetic is worth doing out
loud with a customer comparing this against a no-fee card: a lower headline
cashback rate on a no-fee product can beat a higher one carrying a fee, and the
crossover depends entirely on how much the customer actually spends.
