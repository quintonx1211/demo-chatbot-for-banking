## How the monthly maintenance fee is waived

The Everyday Checking maintenance fee is waived for any statement cycle in which
at least one of the following is true: an average daily balance of $1,500 or
more is maintained across the cycle; qualifying direct deposits totalling $500
or more post to the account; the primary account holder is aged 17 to 24; or the
account is linked to a Premier relationship.

Premier Checking waives its fee at $25,000 in combined deposit balances measured
on the last business day of the cycle, or with a mortgage originated by this bank
in the last 24 months.

An average daily balance is the sum of the end-of-day ledger balances for every
day in the cycle divided by the number of days in the cycle. It is not the same
as the lowest balance during the cycle, and a single large deposit late in the
cycle will not usually satisfy the requirement on its own.

## Order in which items are paid

Items presented against an account on the same business day are paid in this
order: cash withdrawals and teller transactions first, then electronic payments
and cheques in the order the amounts were authorised, smallest to largest.

The order matters when the balance is insufficient to cover every item, because
each item paid into overdraft carries its own fee. Paying smallest first means
more items clear, and the customer pays fewer fees than a largest-first order
would produce.

## Overdraft coverage and the de minimis rule

Overdraft coverage on everyday debit card transactions and ATM withdrawals is
opt-in, and can be added or removed at any time in online banking or by calling
the support centre. Cheques and recurring electronic payments may be paid into
overdraft whether or not the customer has opted in.

No overdraft fee is charged when the account is overdrawn by $5.00 or less at
the end of the business day, and no fee is charged if the account is brought to
a non-negative balance by the end of the next business day. Both conditions are
assessed at end of day, not at the moment the transaction posts.

## Funds availability

Funds from cash and from electronic deposits are available the same business
day. Cheques deposited at a branch before the 4:00 PM ET cut-off are available
the next business day for the first $275, with the remainder available on the
second business day.

A hold of up to seven business days may be placed on a deposit where the account
has been open fewer than 30 days, where the deposit exceeds $6,725 in a single
day, where a cheque has previously been returned unpaid, or where the account
has been overdrawn on six or more banking days in the preceding six months. When
a hold is applied the customer is notified at the time of deposit and told the
date funds will be available.

## Interest and how it is calculated

Interest on savings accounts is calculated on the daily collected balance and
compounded and credited monthly on the last business day of the cycle. The
annual percentage yield assumes interest remains on deposit for a full year; a
withdrawal of interest reduces earnings.

Rates are variable and may change at any time without notice. As at the last
review, the standard savings tier paid 0.40% APY on balances up to $9,999.99,
1.85% APY on balances from $10,000.00 to $99,999.99, and 2.10% APY above that.
Rates are tiered on the whole balance, not marginally: an account holding
$50,000 earns 1.85% on the entire balance, not 0.40% on the first tier.

## Closing an account

An account may be closed at any time by any authorised signatory, in branch,
in writing, or through secure message in online banking. Closure takes effect
once all outstanding items have cleared, which is normally within five business
days but may take up to 30 days where recent cheque deposits are still within
their return window.

The $50 early closure fee applies to accounts closed within 180 days of opening.
It is waived on the death of an account holder, on relocation outside the bank's
service area, and where the closure follows a change of terms the customer did
not accept.

Accrued interest is credited at closure. Any recurring payments set up on the
account must be moved by the customer before closure; the bank cannot redirect
them, and payments presented after closure will be returned unpaid.
