# Card Disputes and Chargebacks

**doc_id:** KB-DISP-001
**owner:** Disputes and Fraud Recovery
**last_reviewed:** 2026-06-15

Two separate rulebooks govern a card dispute and they give different answers.
Federal regulation sets what the customer is entitled to. The card network's
operating rules set what the bank can recover from the merchant. A customer can
be made whole under the first while the bank loses under the second; the
customer is never told to wait for the network process to conclude.

Which federal rule applies depends on the card, not on the transaction:

| Card | Governing rule | Notice deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Debit card, ATM card, prepaid | Regulation E | 60 days from the statement showing the item |
| Credit card | Regulation Z | 60 days from the statement on which the error first appeared |

## Debit card disputes

The investigation is completed within 10 business days of notice. Where it
cannot be, the period extends to 45 calendar days and the account is
provisionally credited for the disputed amount, including any interest, within
10 business days. The customer is told of the provisional credit within 2
business days of it being applied and may use the funds during the
investigation.

For a transaction that was not at a point of sale and did not involve a foreign
currency, the extended period is 45 calendar days. For point-of-sale and foreign
transactions it is 90 calendar days, and for an account open less than 30 days
at the time of the transaction it is 90 calendar days.

If provisional credit is not applied, the investigation must conclude within 10
business days regardless. The bank cannot take the longer period and decline to
credit.

### Customer liability for an unauthorised debit transaction

Liability depends on how quickly the loss is reported, measured from when the
customer learned of the loss or theft, not from when the transaction occurred.

| Reported | Maximum liability |
|---|---|
| Within 2 business days of learning of the loss | $50 |
| After 2 business days but within 60 days of the statement | $500 |
| More than 60 days after the statement was sent | Unlimited for transfers after that point |

The burden of establishing that a transaction was authorised sits with the bank,
not the customer. Where the bank cannot establish authorisation, the transaction
is treated as unauthorised and the error corrected.

The 60-day clock runs from the date the statement was **sent**, not received and
not the transaction date. Where the customer's delay was caused by extenuating
circumstances - an extended hospital stay or travel - the period is extended by
a reasonable time.

## Credit card disputes

A billing error notice must reach the bank within 60 days of the statement on
which the error first appeared. The bank acknowledges within 30 days and
resolves within two complete billing cycles, and in no case more than 90 days.

The disputed amount, and any finance charge on it, need not be paid while the
dispute is open, and the account may not be reported delinquent on that amount
to a credit bureau during that period. The rest of the balance remains payable.

Maximum liability for unauthorised use of a credit card is $50, and is zero
where the card was not present and the number alone was used.

## Network chargeback rules

The chargeback is the bank's mechanism for recovering the money from the
merchant's bank. It runs to the network's calendar and its deadlines are
absolute - a missed deadline loses the case with no appeal, which is why a
dispute is filed on the day it is reported rather than at the end of an internal
review.

| Stage | Time limit |
|---|---|
| Cardholder dispute filed | Up to 120 calendar days from the transaction or expected delivery date |
| Merchant response | 30 calendar days from the chargeback |
| Pre-arbitration | 30 calendar days |
| Escalation to arbitration | 10 calendar days |

The 120-day limit runs from the expected delivery date where goods or services
were to be delivered later, which is why a dispute over an undelivered order
placed eight months ago may still be within time. Certain fraud codes run to 540
days. A small number of codes run from the date the fraud was identified by the
network's fraud programme rather than from the transaction.

Reason codes fall into four families: fraud (10.x), authorisation (11.x),
processing errors (12.x) and consumer disputes (13.x). The family determines the
evidence the merchant must produce, and therefore how likely recovery is.

## Before a dispute is raised

A customer who does not recognise a transaction is asked to check three things
first, because each accounts for a material share of items reported as fraud and
later withdrawn: the merchant's trading name may differ from its shop sign; a
subscription may have renewed on its anniversary; and a family member with a
supplementary card may have made the purchase.

Where the customer has already contacted the merchant and been refused, that is
recorded and the dispute proceeds without further delay. Contacting the merchant
first is not a precondition for a debit card dispute and the customer is never
told it is.

## Provisional credit reversal

Where the investigation finds no error, provisional credit is reversed. The
customer is given 5 business days' notice before the debit, and the bank honours
cheques and preauthorised transfers that would have cleared had the credit
remained, without charge, for 5 business days after that notice.
