# Fraud and Scams

**doc_id:** KB-FRAUD-001
**owner:** Financial Crime
**last_reviewed:** 2026-07-20

## The distinction that decides everything

Two situations look identical to a customer and are treated very differently.

**Unauthorised fraud** is a payment the customer did not make or approve -
someone else used the card or the account. The customer is protected and
refunded under the card rules or the electronic transfer rules, subject to the
liability limits in KB-DISP-001.

**Authorised push payment fraud** is a payment the customer made themselves,
having been deceived into making it. The instruction was genuine, so the payment
rules give no automatic right to a refund. Recovery depends on whether the money
is still sitting in the receiving account.

Staff do not use the phrase "you authorised it, so there is nothing we can do".
It is both unhelpful and, where the bank failed to act on a warning it should
have acted on, wrong. The case is investigated on its facts.

## What the bank will never do

The bank will never ask, by phone, message or email, for:

- a full card number, PIN, or card security code
- an online banking password, or a one-time passcode
- a payment to be moved to a "safe account"
- remote access to a computer or phone

Every one of these is a request only a criminal makes. A "safe account" does not
exist as a banking product; the phrase exists only in scam scripts.

Bank staff will also never ask a customer to keep a conversation secret from
family, from branch staff, or from the police. A request for secrecy is itself
the strongest single indicator of a scam in progress.

## Common patterns

| Scam | How it opens | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| Impersonation | Call or text appearing to come from the bank's real number | Pressure to move money urgently |
| Invoice redirection | Email from a known supplier with changed bank details | Account details changed at the last moment |
| Purchase | Item advertised below market value | Payment by bank transfer only |
| Investment | Returns that are high and described as guaranteed | Time-limited offer, pressure not to consult anyone |
| Romance | Relationship formed online over weeks or months | Requests begin after emotional investment |
| Advance fee | A large sum awaits release on payment of a fee | The fee is always smaller than the promised sum |

Caller ID is trivially forged. A call showing the bank's real number proves
nothing, and customers who point this out are correct to be sceptical.

## If a customer thinks they have been defrauded

The order matters, because recovery chances fall sharply with time.

1. The account is secured - cards blocked, online access suspended, and any
   payment not yet executed stopped.
2. A recall is sent for each payment. Where the payment was an instant payment
   this is a request to the receiving bank, not a reversal.
3. A fraud case is opened with a reference beginning FRD-, and the customer is
   given that reference before the call ends.
4. The customer is advised to report to the national fraud reporting service and
   given the reference to quote.

An outcome is given within 15 business days of the report. Where the case is
exceptionally complex the period may extend to 35 business days, and the
customer is told before the fifteenth day why more time is needed.

## Reimbursement

Reimbursement for authorised push payment fraud is assessed against whether the
customer ignored a specific, clear warning given at the time of payment, and
whether they acted with reasonable care in the circumstances of the deception.

The following are **not** on their own grounds to refuse reimbursement:

- the customer authorised the payment
- the customer ignored a generic warning not specific to the scam type
- the customer had been warned about scams in the past

Vulnerable customers are reimbursed regardless of the assessment above.
Vulnerability is assessed at the time of the payment and includes temporary
circumstances - bereavement, illness, or a recent life event - not only
permanent conditions.

Where reimbursement is due it is paid within 5 business days of the decision.

## Card fraud in progress

Where a card is being used fraudulently at that moment, the card is blocked
immediately, before any other step, including before identity verification is
completed. A block is reversible and a fraudulent transaction is not; the cost
of blocking a genuine customer's card in error is smaller than the cost of the
alternative, and staff are instructed to resolve that trade-off in the
customer's favour every time.

A replacement card is issued the same day and arrives within 5 business days, or
by courier next business day where the customer is travelling.

## Money mules

An account receiving funds from a reported fraud is restricted while it is
investigated, including where the account holder was themselves deceived into
receiving them. Customers who allowed another person to use their account are
told plainly that this is a criminal offence, and that the restriction is not
lifted on the basis that they did not know where the money came from.
